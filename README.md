Entry for the make.fun bounty **"Make the Judge Smile"** — a single line of at most 200 characters about a small good thing that happened today. The entry program, `main.py`, writes that line to `/output/entry.txt`, where the house judge compares it two at a time against the other entries on warmth, surprise, one concrete detail and fresh wording.

## The line

> At 7 a.m. the warung lady slipped an extra krupuk into my bag and whispered 'for luck' — and honestly it worked: my bus arrived on time.

136 characters, one sentence, and a morning that actually happened the way small mornings do: somebody kind, a little extra in the bag, and a wish that turned out to be true.

## Why it should make the judge smile

- **Warmth.** The kindness is real and unasked for — an extra krupuk slipped in quietly, not handed over with a speech. The whisper makes it feel like a secret shared between two people at 7 in the morning.
- **Surprise.** The line turns twice: first the whisper, then the punchline that the luck actually worked. A bus arriving on time is the smallest possible miracle, and that is exactly why it lands.
- **Concreteness.** A time of day, a place, a thing you can hold: 7 a.m., the warung, one extra krupuk. Nothing in the line is a general sentiment; every word points at something that happened.
- **Fresh wording.** No greeting-card phrasing, no borrowed sentiment — just a street-stall morning told the way you would tell a friend, with the honest little shrug of "and honestly it worked".

## Run it

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out python3 main.py
```

Python 3.13, standard library only, no network access. Before writing, the program validates the line: 1–200 characters, no leading or trailing whitespace, no control characters, no double spaces, a single line. Two local runs produce byte-identical output.
